Technical Program Manager Epic Games

Active Directory Domain Migration

I led the move of 150 employees' logins and computers into one company-wide system for managing access. I built the process and training that let the team run sessions independently, so we hit our 6-month timeline.

Show details
PROBLEM
  • Each region ran its own separate system for employee logins and computer access. These systems are called Active Directory domains.
  • The team tracked who needed to move using one-time data exports. The exports were often out of date and didn't match each other.
  • It was unclear who needed to be moved and what kind of move they needed.
  • The steps for a migration lived mostly in people's heads, so engineers couldn't lead sessions on their own.
  • Employees didn't know what to expect in their session. Early on, 3 sessions had to be rescheduled because people weren't prepared.
WHAT NEEDED TO BE DONE
  • Move 150 employees across America, the UK, China and South Korea. About half of them had 2-3 computers or devices each.
  • Move their accounts and computers from the regional systems into one central company-wide system.
  • Make access management consistent, so future security and access projects could move forward.
WHAT I DID
  • Built one tracking sheet and updated it after every migration session, instead of pulling new exports.
  • Cut the tracked information from 25 columns to 7, keeping only what we needed to prepare for each migration.
  • Wrote a step-by-step runbook so every migration followed the same process.
  • After the first 3 reschedules, made a short slide deck that told employees what to expect and what to do during their session.
IMPACT
  • Migrated all 150 employees in 6 months, hitting our timeline.
  • All 3 engineers could run sessions on their own using the runbook. They could run sessions in parallel without needing the lead (most senior) engineer in each one, which scaled our workflow and sped things up.
  • Reschedules caused by lack of preparation dropped from 3 to 0 after the slide deck. The few later reschedules came from scheduling conflicts we couldn't control.
Technical Project Manager Activision

Player Flags MVP

I led a new “Player Flags” service from idea to launch, on time for a major game release. It let four teams manage player restrictions without getting in each other’s way.

Show details
PROBLEM
  • Player restrictions were built into one big, tangled system. These ranged from limiting a player to matches against computer opponents, to banning an account for a set period or permanently
  • 4 teams relied on it: Anti-Cheat, Moderation, Matchmaking, and Player Support
  • Any change by one team risked breaking the others’ work
  • Moderation had outgrown the old system and couldn’t scale on top of it
WHAT NEEDED TO BE DONE
  • Activision was breaking its big system into smaller, independent services, so each could be updated without affecting the rest
  • This project would move player restrictions into their own service, called Player Flags, that other teams could connect to easily
  • A first working version had to be ready by the next game’s launch date
WHAT I DID
  • Interviewed the 4 teams to learn how they worked and what frustrated them
  • Turned what I heard into a clear list of requirements
  • Cut scope to hit the deadline: kept the essentials and moved large batch actions (restricting up to tens of thousands of players at once) to after launch
  • Got engineering and all 4 teams to agree on who owned what, who depended on whom, and how we’d test together
  • Noticed the team starting to build beyond the first version and steered them back to the plan
IMPACT
  • Shipped on time for the game's launch
  • Finished early enough to start the next phase ahead of schedule
  • Delivered a real step in Activision’s move to smaller, independent services
  • The 4 teams gave positive feedback on the new service
  • Batch actions later slowed down everyday requests, which confirmed that holding them back until after launch was the right call

Closing the Engineering Team Knowledge Gap

I closed a knowledge gap between our US and Dublin engineering teams. Problems that used to wait a day or more now got fixed in hours, sometimes minutes, which senior leadership confirmed.

Show details
PROBLEM
  • Our team ran 7 services. The Dublin team (6 engineers) knew 5 of them, but the US team (3 engineers) knew only 2
  • Problems on those 5 services during US working hours sat untouched until Dublin came online, sometimes a full day or a whole weekend
  • Dublin engineers were also woken up after hours for routine issues, not real emergencies, only because they were the only ones who could fix them
WHAT NEEDED TO BE DONE
  • Get the US team able to diagnose and fix all 7 services on their own
WHAT I DID
  • Paired US and Dublin engineers on shared projects so knowledge spread naturally

  • Built one central, standardized guide in Confluence (our team’s shared wiki) covering each service
IMPACT
  • In about 6 months, the US team could handle all 7 services independently
  • Response time dropped from a day or a weekend to hours or even minutes
  • Dublin’s after-hours alerts for routine issues stopped
  • Senior leadership confirmed the improvement after checking with directors at other studios

Putting Quality Before the Deadline

Show details

  • Help the team decide between hitting the date and protecting players at launch

Show details
PROBLEM

  • The business wanted to sell mirrors directly to customers for use at home

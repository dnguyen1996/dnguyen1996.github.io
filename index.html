Technical Project Manager Activision

Player Flags MVP

PROBLEM
  • Feature Bans lived inside the platform's old monolith architecture
  • 4 teams depended on it: Anti-Cheat, Moderation, Matchmaking, and Player Support
  • Because it was part of the monolith, no team could change it without risking every other team's service
  • Moderation was hitting a bottleneck trying to scale on top of it
WHAT NEEDED TO BE DONE
  • Activision was moving away from the monolith toward microservices, so each service could scale and update on its own
  • This project would pull player state out of the monolith into its own service, called Player Flags, with a clean API
  • The new service needed an MVP that could ship by the next game's launch date
WHAT I DID
  • Interviewed the 4 customer teams to learn their workflows and pain points
  • Turned those findings into clear requirements
  • Cut scope to fit the deadline: kept the core functionality, pushed bulk operations to after launch
  • Aligned engineering and all 4 teams on who owned what, what depended on what, and how we'd test it together
  • Caught the team building past MVP scope partway through and redirected them back to the plan
IMPACT
  • Shipped on time for the game's launch
  • Had enough runway left over to start the next phase of work early
  • Delivered a real, working piece of Activision's move to microservices
  • Customer teams gave positive feedback on the new service
  • Bulk operations did slow down real-time requests later, confirming it was right to push them past launch

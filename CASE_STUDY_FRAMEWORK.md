## The nine sections

Aim for 500 to 800 words per case study. Sections 5 and 8 are what a resume does not have.

1. **Headline** — the result in one line, with a number and a timeframe.
2. **Snapshot** — company, role, core team, partner teams, duration, who was affected.
3. **The problem** — what was broken, who it hurt, what it would cost if left alone.
4. **What made it hard** — the one or two real constraints.
5. **Key decisions** — two or three, each written as Options / The call / Why / Pushback / Proof.
6. **How it ran** — phases with durations, plus one thing that went wrong and the adjustment.
7. **Outcome** — measured results, then what happened afterward. Label anything projected or untracked.
8. **What I'd do differently** — one or two specific changes.
9. **Artifacts** — recreated, sanitized versions of real documents (roadmap, scope table, risk log). Never the originals.

## Questions to answer for each new case study

1. Team makeup and duration?
2. For each decision: what were the options, and what did you give up?
3. Who pushed back, and how was it resolved?
4. What later showed the call was right or wrong?
5. What were the phases and how long was each?
6. What went wrong along the way, and what did you change?
7. What would you do differently?
8. Which documents did the team use day to day?

## Writing rules

- Plain language a non-technical recruiter can follow.
- "I" for what you did, "we" for what the team did.
- No internal code names, customer names or exact financials.
- Nothing invented: every claim comes from what actually happened.
